# Lesson 2 - The rules you work under
*Position · about 35 minutes · artefact: `position/rules.md`*

## What you are making
A one page rules card you keep next to your keyboard: what never goes into a prompt, what your employer allows, who owns what you make, and how you verify.

## Why it matters
Most AI incidents at work are not clever attacks. They are a person pasting the wrong thing into a chat window on a Friday. A rules card written in your own words is the cheapest defence that exists.

## The demo
The coach takes a realistic paragraph containing a client name, a phone number, an address and a fee, and redacts it live: names to roles, numbers to ranges, addresses to regions. Then it shows the same paragraph unredacted and asks the student what a data breach would look like if that had been pasted.

## Your turn
1. Ask your employer or read your contract for the AI-use rules. If there are none, write "no policy: I follow the strictest reading" and say so.
2. Fill in `position/rules.md` under the four headings: data I never paste, what my employer says, who owns what I make, how I verify.
3. Do one redaction pass of your own on a real document with the personal details changed, and save the before and after in `position/`.

## The check
1. The card has all four headings, in your words.
2. The redaction example shows a real before and after, with at least three categories of detail changed.
3. No credentials, client names or personal data are stored in the file itself.

Failure signs: copy-pasted policy language with nothing of your own, or a redaction that only deletes a surname.

## Sealed when
`position/rules.md` is complete, the redaction pair exists, and nothing sensitive lives in the repository or folder.
